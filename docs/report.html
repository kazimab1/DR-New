Stage B closed

Nine runs, every ablation lost or tied, and the designed recipe stood

The frozen configuration is 512 px · EfficientNet-B0 · focal-ordinal head · stratified exposure · no eye-pair fusion — which is exactly the default specified in docs/03_model_architecture.md before any run happened. That is a legitimate reportable outcome rather than a null result: the ablations were run, two of them moved real quantities, and the design still won.

Two levers mattered and three did not, and the separation is not close. Reading effect sizes against each metric's own noise floor is what makes them comparable — a 0.087 change in macro-F1 and a 0.123 change in grade-1 recall are not the same size until you divide by what each metric does under reseeding alone.

Per-metric noise floors come from B1's observed spread across 384/512/768: QWK 0.0276, macro-F1 0.0144, grade-1 F1 0.0190, grade-1 recall 0.0220, MAE 0.0230. Experiment B3 · output head

Focal weighting demonstrably does its stated job

512 px, EfficientNet-B0, stratified exposure, no fusion. The ordinal_focal arm is the B1 512 run, so B3 cost two new runs. This is the first Stage B experiment where the choice moved anything: QWK spans 0.053 across the three heads, nearly twice the 0.028 B1 produced across every resolution.

ordinal and ordinal_focal differ only in γ, so the pair isolates focal weighting exactly — same head, same loss family, one hyperparameter.
HeadQWKMacro-F1 Grade-1 F1G1 recallG1 prec. MAEGPU-min
softmax_ce0.6470.4880.159 0.1820.1410.41618.9
ordinal (γ=0)0.7000.3980.122 0.1480.1040.38916.3
ordinal_focal (γ=2)0.6790.4590.142 0.2710.0960.41518.1

  1. Softmax CE is ruled out on two counts

    It gives up 0.053 QWK against ordinal — 1.9× the QWK floor — and it discards the CORAL structure that Phase 7's reasoner needs. Its macro-F1 and grade-1 precision are the best of the three, but neither is the stated criterion, and neither survives losing the ordinal head.

  2. Focal costs QWK inside noise and buys grade 1 far outside it

    γ=2 against γ=0: QWK −0.021 (0.8× floor, inside noise), grade-1 recall +0.123 — +83%, at 5.6× its floor — and macro-F1 +0.061 at 4.2×. Focal loss was included to stop the rare grades being drowned by grade 0, and that is precisely and measurably what it did.

Decision

Focal-ordinal, γ=2. A QWK cost inside the noise floor in exchange for an 83% gain in grade-1 recall is the trade this project wants: the grader exists to be disagreed with, and a grader that cannot see grade 1 at all gives the evidence pathway nothing to disagree about.

A two-way experiment that was specified as a three-way

stratified_exposure is the B1 512 run. The third row is not a result — see below.

The third arm was a duplicate

class_balanced returned metrics identical to the baseline on every figure — QWK, macro-F1, grade-1 F1, grade-1 recall, MAE and distinct predictions, to the last recorded digit. Not coincidence: in make_sampler both weighted strategies weight by inverse grade frequency and differ only in draws per epoch, which defaults to len(grades) for both. With epoch_samples unset they are one sampler under two names, and on the same seed they emit the same index sequence — verified by comparing the two index lists directly.

Nothing downstream is invalidated: B1–B3 all used stratified_exposure, which is a real sampler. But B4 tested two options, not three, and it cost 18.8 GPU-minutes to learn that. make_sampler now warns when the two coincide and tests/test_grading.py pins the equivalence.

On the comparison that did run, weighting wins clearly: macro-F1 +0.087 (6.0× floor), MAE −0.064 (2.8×), QWK +0.039 (1.4×).

But weighting does not help grade 1. Grade-1 F1 +0.017 (0.9×) and recall +0.008 (0.4×) are both inside noise. Read against B3 the division of labour is clean, and it is worth stating in the write-up: focal weighting moves grade 1; the sampler moves overall class balance. They are not substitutes, and neither substitutes for the other's job.

Experiment B5 · eye-pair fusion

The best grader in Stage B, rejected on purpose

Phase 6 projections use each arm's own measured throughput.

single image0.6790.4590.142 0.2710.41518.120.9 h

Fusion takes top QWK, macro-F1, grade-1 recall and MAE. It is rejected anyway, on three grounds, and the first is the one that matters.

  1. It confounds the signal the whole thesis rests on

    With fusion, M1's grade for image X is a function of X and its fellow eye, while M2's evidence is a function of X alone. When they disagree, that can now mean "the fellow eye carried disease M2 cannot see in this image" rather than "the grade contradicts the evidence here". DR is frequently asymmetric, so this is not a corner case: it would systematically inflate disagreement in exactly the cases the referral rule flags, and those flags would not be errors.

  2. The budget cannot absorb it

    21.1 img/s against 46.0 puts Phase 6 at 45.5 GPU-hours — over the ~20 h plan and over a full 30 h weekly quota.

  3. The evidence is thin for the criterion as specified

    B5 was specified as paired across seeds; one seed per arm was run. ΔQWK is 1.2× its floor, grade-1 F1 0.5× and MAE 1.0× — both inside noise.

Decision

No fusion. If this thesis were about grading accuracy, fusion would win. It is not, and buying 0.034 QWK by contaminating the referral signal is the one trade CLAUDE.md explicitly forbids.

Experiment A0 · data audit

Closed: pass, with two limitations that belong in the thesis

A0 checks that the cache and splits are what they claim to be, before any number computed on them can be trusted. It passed, but two findings need stating rather than filing.

The EyePACS crop alarm was a false one

retinal_bbox reported a full-frame fallback rate of 0.99919 against a 0.005 gate — apparently a total failure of retinal cropping. It is benign: the Kaggle mirror ships images already cropped, so "nothing to crop" is the correct answer and the gate is the wrong test for a pre-cropped source. Confirmed by measuring 260 crops rather than eyeballing them (EyePACS 100, APTOS 60, DDR 60, IDRiD 40): retinal-width-to-frame median 1.000, zero tiles below 0.75, lit fractions 0.70–0.79 against π/4 = 0.785 for a disc inscribed in a square.

EyePACS is 693 images short, and not at random

The cache holds 88 009 of the official 88 702 — 0.78% missing. It is upstream of this project: cache_report.json shows found = cached = 88 009 with 0 failures, so the mirror never had them. APTOS, Messidor-2 and DDR reconcile exactly, every grade (DDR's 12 522 being the published 13 673 less 1 151 ungradable).

The loss is non-random by grade — χ² = 105.6 on 4 df, with grade 4 lost at three times grade 0's rate. The likely mechanism is that mirrors drop unreadable files and severe DR correlates with media opacity, which makes it a mild severity-dependent selection effect rather than random attrition. Prevalence impact is negligible (rDR 19.34% → 19.23%), but a severity-dependent one is exactly the kind a referral study should declare.

Structure · passed

1.989 images per patient — close enough to 2.0 that the ^(\d+)_(left|right)$ patient parsing is working; anything near 1.0 would mean every downstream number was inflated by leakage. Split fractions exact, held-out rows identical across variants, no balancing shortfall.

Outstanding: the Messidor-2 visual audit, which stays locked until unblinding. Its counts already reconcile exactly.

Phase 4 · Stage C

The evidence pathway: code complete, C1 unblocked mid-flight

Stage C builds M2 — the half of the system that looks for lesions rather than grading the image. Its independence from M1 is the contribution, not an implementation detail: if the two shared an encoder they would fail together, and disagreement between them would carry no information at all. A static AST test enforces that evidence.py never imports grading.py.

  • C1 Optic disc and fovea regressor (M2b)FAILS the gate at 0.686 DD — but the fovea passes alone and the disc error is heavy-tailed. See below. done · 1.3 min
  • C2 Four-channel lesion segmenter (M2a)Microaneurysm, haemorrhage, hard exudate, soft exudate · per-lesion Dice and IoU ~50 min
  • C3 Cross-domain transferDDR → IDRiD and back. Whether the evidence pathway means anything off-domain. 2 runs · ~60 min
  • C4 Evidence-only gradingBlocked: needs the M3 reasoner, which Phase 4 does not build deferred
Why only four lesion channels

Venous beading, IRMA and neovascularisation have no pixel annotations on any Kaggle-reachable dataset. M3 declares them unobservable rather than treating absence of evidence as evidence of absence — which matters, because those three are precisely the features that separate severe NPDR from proliferative disease.

Read C2's Dice over annotated images only

Most fundus images carry no soft exudates. Averaging Dice over every image folds in a long run of empty-target/empty-prediction pairs that score 1.0 by convention, so the headline rises without the model having segmented anything. The reported figure is dice_present — the mean over images where the lesion is actually annotated — with dice_all kept beside it so the gap is visible rather than assumed. Over-segmentation is instrumented separately, because a model that finds lesions everywhere agrees with every grade and makes disagreement meaningless.

C1 was blocked · cause found, fixed, verified

The first C1 run had no training targets at all, and every pipeline stage before it had reported success. IDRiD ships two image sets whose names look almost identical: Part A (81 images with lesion masks, IDRiD_01–81) and Part B (516 graded images, IDRiD_001–516), and the Part C optic-disc and fovea tables cover Part B. IDRiD_01 is not IDRiD_001, so a Part A-only cache joins to neither.

On top of that, the Phase 2 notebook resolved the coordinate source directory by searching the path for "segmentation" — which pins it to Part A. Every coordinate row missed, and the --no-grades fallback then wrote a perfectly well-formed manifest with an empty geometry column. Nothing looked wrong anywhere.

Fixed at three levels: a new top-up notebook caches Part B beside Part A's masks; Phase 2 now resolves the directory by matching the coordinate tables' own IDs and prints the match counts; and prepare_manifest.py exits non-zero without writing when --coords projects nothing. Verified on a fixture reproducing the real directory layout: the old discovery resolved 0 of 50 coordinate rows, the new one 50 of 50, after which C1 trained end to end.

Experiment C1 · optic disc and fovea

Fails the gate at 0.686 DD — and the single number hides what happened

413 IDRiD Part B images, 330 train / 83 validation, 512 px, early-stopped from epoch 6 after 17 epochs. 1.3 GPU-minutes. Error is reported in disc diameters rather than pixels, because a fixed pixel tolerance means different things at different fields of view; IDRiD publishes no disc diameter, so it is derived per image from the disc-to-fovea distance ÷ 2.5.

  1. The fovea passes on its own; only the disc misses

    Fovea 0.289 DD against disc 1.082. They are two outputs of one head and the failure is not shared — which matters, because a macula-centred coordinate frame needs the fovea and the 4-2-1 quadrant rule needs both.

  2. The disc error is heavy-tailed, by a factor of 2.3

    Mean 62.0 px against median 26.6 px. On the median image the disc lands at 0.354 DD — inside the gate. A minority of images are far enough out to carry the mean past it. "Most images slightly off" and "a few placed somewhere else entirely" have different causes and different fixes, and no aggregate separates them.

  3. It is not the failure the gate was built to catch

    It beats the constant predictor by 0.754 DD (1.440 → 0.686) and lifts landmarks within 0.5 DD from 11.4% to 74.7%. Fundus framing is stereotyped enough that a constant predictor is a real opponent; this model clears it decisively, so it has learnt where landmarks are rather than where they usually are.

  4. It overfits early, and stops on the wrong landmark

    Train loss falls 0.084 → 0.014 while validation loss sits at ~0.030 from epoch 6 — expected on 330 images. Early stopping then halted while the fovea was still improving (36.4 → 18.1 px), because the disc term dominates the stopping metric.

Recorded result

C1 FAILS. The criterion was fixed before the run and it is a mean over both landmarks. The median is reported beside it as a diagnostic and is not a second criterion — swapping in the statistic that reads better once the result is known is precisely what the pre-registration exists to prevent.

What happens next

train_geometry.py now writes val_errors.csv: every validation image with predicted and true coordinates, per-landmark error in pixels and disc diameters, sorted worst disc error first. It also flags side_flipped — the disc predicted on the wrong side of the fovea. The disc sits nasal to the macula, so a sign flip there is a laterality error rather than an imprecise one.

That distinction decides M3's reasoning. If the tail is mostly laterality, it has one systematic cause and quadrant reasoning survives a fix. If the disc is simply imprecise across the tail, M3 falls back to count-only rules — which docs/00_START_HERE.md names as the designed fallback, not a failure to hide.

Method · a recurring failure shape

Every serious bug so far produced a well-formed artefact

Not one of these announced itself. In each case a stage that could not do its job still emitted something structurally valid, and the run exited zero. They are listed unordered because they are not a sequence — they are the same defect recurring in different places, which is what makes it worth a paragraph in the methods chapter rather than a footnote.

  • The image counter counted the wrong thing

    glob("*") matched direct children only, so nested EyePACS reported 1 cached image where there were 88 009. The cache was fine; the number was not.

    Signal: a count too round and too small to be real.

  • Cache repathing kept a fixed number of path components

    Three trailing components is right for most datasets and wrong for EyePACS, which is five levels deep. Paths were silently truncated until every image failed to open. Now anchored on the <dataset>/images boundary instead of a count.

    Signal: exit 1 — the one failure here that was loud.

  • Resume loaded a 768 px checkpoint into a 384 px run

    Shapes happened to be compatible, so training continued from weights fitted at a different resolution and reported plausible numbers. A hard architecture guard now refuses the load.

    Signal: none. Caught by asking what resume could not detect.

  • The verdict cell endorsed a collapsed model

    A model predicting grade 1 for every image scores 1.000 grade-1 recall, and the summary read that as "the class is being learned". distinct_predictions is now checked first: when it is 1, the run decides nothing.

    Signal: a perfect score on a hard metric.

  • Two sampler names, one sampler

    B4's third arm was byte-identical to its baseline and cost 18.8 GPU-minutes to discover. A three-way experiment had been a two-way all along.

    Signal: two arms agreeing to the last decimal on six metrics.

  • Dice punished a correct empty prediction

    At 512 px an empty channel predicted empty — the right answer — scored ~0.99 loss, drowning the channels that carried signal. Empty (sample, channel) pairs are now excluded from Dice and carried by BCE.

    Signal: loss dominated by the easiest cases in the batch.

  • Two lesion channels printed under one label

    Haemorrhage and hard exudate both truncate to HA, so the per-epoch log showed two channels under one name and a collapsed channel could hide behind a healthy one. Channels are now labelled MA/HE/EX/SE.

    Signal: a duplicate label in a log nobody reads closely.

  • A manifest written with an empty geometry column

    The C1 blocker above: coordinates projected for zero images, and a valid manifest was written anyway. This is the same shape as all the others, and the reason the fix was made at three levels rather than one.

    Signal: a downstream stage with nothing to train on.
The countermeasure that keeps working

Gate on the quantity the next stage actually consumes, not the quantity this stage happens to produce — images carrying coordinates rather than images cached, mask files on disk rather than mask directories found, distinct grades emitted rather than recall scored — and make a zero there fatal rather than merely printed. Every fix above is an instance of that one rule.

What these numbers can and cannot claim

Limitation · B1

B1 sweeps 384 / 512 / 768 px but every run reads the same 512 px cache, so it cannot test the claim that motivated it — that a microaneurysm is 10–20 px at native resolution and vanishes under downsampling. The 768 arm upsamples 512 px data and carries no additional information; consistent with that, its recall was unchanged and only its precision improved. Testing the real claim needs a cache rebuilt at 768 px from the originals: another full Phase 1 pass at ~2.25× the storage. Not done and not planned. Report B1 as choosing an operating point under a fixed 512 px cache, not as a resolution-sensitivity study.

Why grade-1 F1, and not QWK or recall

Quadratic weighting barely punishes a grade-1 error, because grade 1 sits one step from grade 0: a model that never predicts grade 1 at all still scores about 0.97 QWK on a realistic distribution. Grade-1 recall fails the opposite way — predict grade 1 for everything and score 1.000. Both failures are pinned in tests/test_grading.py, and every run reports how many distinct grades it emits so a collapsed run is discarded rather than read.

Stage B numbers are not results

Stage B selects a recipe; it does not produce findings. The checkpoints are discarded and Phase 6 retrains from scratch. QWK on this custom patient-grouped split is never comparable to the Kaggle leaderboard — the only comparable figure is H1, on the official test split, reported beside the 0.8496 reference.

The grade-1 weakness is the mechanism, not a defect

Grade-1 F1 sits near 0.15 under every configuration tried, and Stage B is now closed with that unchanged. It says the grader cannot reliably see microaneurysms — which is exactly the case this thesis is built to exploit. The evidence pathway segments microaneurysms directly and independently. A grader blind to grade 1, paired with an evidence path that is not, is precisely where disagreement should fire. That belongs in the write-up as the mechanism working, not buried as a weak number.

What would falsify the approach

C3 is the experiment to watch. If per-lesion Dice collapses when the segmenter moves between DDR and IDRiD, then disagreement measured on one source says little about another, and the referral rule does not generalise. That would be a finding to report plainly rather than a result to tune away — and it is why C3 is in the notebook rather than deferred.
